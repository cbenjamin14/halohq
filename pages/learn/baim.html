The Ultimate Guide to Aiming and Accuracy in Halo Infinite

Mastering Aiming and Accuracy in Halo Infinite

Last Updated: October 14, 2025

In Halo Infinite, few skills matter more than precise aiming and consistent accuracy. Whether you play on PC with a mouse and keyboard or on console with a controller, your ability to land shots determines how often you win fights. This in-depth guide explores every aspect of aiming—from sensitivity and muscle memory to drills and mental focus—so you can hit your targets with confidence every match.

Overview

Aiming in Halo Infinite is a blend of mechanical skill, spatial awareness, and prediction. The game’s sandbox design rewards steady accuracy over panic firing. Because every weapon has a unique recoil pattern, bullet magnetism, and optimal range, you need to understand how each behaves.

Understanding the Aim Assist System

Controller players benefit from aim assist—a subtle magnetic pull that helps track targets. It is powerful at close to mid-range, but cannot replace good crosshair discipline. Overreliance leads to bad habits, especially with precision weapons like the BR75 or Sidekick.

Mouse and Keyboard Precision

On PC, accuracy is purely mechanical. Mouse players rely on sensitivity settings, muscle memory, and smooth hand movements. Because there’s no aim assist, every shot must be earned through practice.

Finding Your Perfect Sensitivity

Your sensitivity determines how you interact with the game world. Start at a medium setting—around 4–5 on controller or 800 DPI with 1.5 in-game sensitivity for mouse—and adjust gradually.

Field of View (FOV) and Target Visibility

FOV affects battlefield visibility. Higher FOV increases awareness but can make targets smaller. Experiment until you find a balance between visibility and control.

Warm-Up Drills

Spend 5–10 minutes in the Academy’s Weapon Drills focusing on your top three weapons: BR75, Sidekick, and Commando. Practice smooth tracking and rapid snapping between targets.

Crosshair Placement

Always keep your reticle at head level to minimize wasted movement when enemies appear.

Recoil Control and Burst Firing

Learn each weapon’s rhythm. Controlled bursts improve accuracy, while continuous firing increases bloom and spread.

Tracking vs. Flicking

Tracking is following a moving target smoothly; flicking is snapping your aim quickly. Master both for different weapon types.

Controller-Specific Tips

Adjust look acceleration and deadzone. Recommended: look acceleration 3–4, sensitivity 4–6. Maintain a comfortable grip and smooth thumb motion.

PC-Specific Tips

Focus on smooth mouse control. Keep your elbow on the desk, use your arm for large motions, wrist for fine corrections. 800 DPI and 1000Hz polling rate are common standards.

Weapon-Specific Accuracy Practice

Practice individually with key weapons: Battle Rifle for headshots, Commando for recoil control, Sidekick for rhythm. Cycle drills per weapon.

Body Positioning and Strafing

Practice counter-strafing to remain harder to hit while keeping aim steady.

Using Cover to Stabilize Aim

Peek from cover for deliberate, well-aimed shots.

Mental Focus and Consistency

Avoid frustration and fatigue. Short, focused sessions improve muscle memory.

Analyzing Gameplay Footage

Record and review matches to identify aim errors and patterns.

Custom Games for Target Practice

Create private lobbies with bots to practice weapon skills, strafing, and aiming under pressure.

Routine for Continuous Improvement

Use a repeatable loop: warm-up drills, matches, analysis. Consistency beats randomness.

Combining Aim with Game Sense

Perfect aim alone doesn’t win games; smart positioning, awareness of shields, and timing power weapons enhance effectiveness.

Final Thoughts

Improving aim takes time, structure, and awareness. Consistency and discipline are key, whether on PC or console. Develop muscle memory and refine settings for maximum performance.
